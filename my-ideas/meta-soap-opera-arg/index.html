Seasons

So here's the list of seasons I wrote

I don't even remember what the heck was going on in my head to type out such a cohesive outline for even just the meta aspect over so many seasons

There will be many more, but yeah

  • Season 1: Happens like a standard soap-opera, although now I've come back here and decided the person who will start to unravel the show's reälity will be someone new in town, and essentially the decisive protagonist to most of the fanbase (at least as long as they're around). Around the middle of the season, someone will comment on something that seems to be a cliché trope in soap-operas, but nobody will think too much about it. In the penultimate episode however, something dramatic will happen, and during an overdrawn suspenseful revelation, they'll remark saying that everyone should stop acting like ning-nongs and get to the point, and then just blurts out the point. At the end of the final episode, they'll mutter something along the lines of: "Why does everything feel so strange? This feels like a soap-opera..."
  • Season 2: They don't think much about it other than that it's strange, but eventually they do tell someone about what they've felt. It takes a while for the other person to really understand and believe them, and they're also interrupted, which only gives them more conviction that something really weird is happening. But it isn't long before the other person starts telling people about how the first person is starting to feel pretty strange - from both perspectives between them and the rest of the cast. They feel rejected, but quickly snap out of it and try to tell another person - who shortly decides they're a bit wacko. Now they start to feel a combination of desperation and rejection, but not much happens other than that.
  • Season 3: Something shocking happens - something way too fitting for a soap-opera, rather than reälity - and it takes a while for the person to learn about it. They're still having the frustrations of slow-moving dialogue, almost blatantly inhibitory, but eventually they learn about it, and start trying to tell people on the internet about it toward the end of the season. This doesn't currently connect with reälity, though.
  • Season 4: They start trying to connect with the other people and really make the whole cast aware that something's definitely awry. At first, they're like, "wow, are you still on about that weird idea?"...but one other person starts to realise. They keep getting interrupted when they try to talk to each other alone, but get the chance once they're too suspicious. Eventually they mention superstition and that it might just be that reälity is a really weird thing - but they agree to keep sharing their observations with each other.
  • Season 5: Eventually, one of them starts doing research on soap-operas and related tropes - and gets that feel like when you've just done a personality test/astrological assessment/whatever and are now reading remarkably accurate articles about your pathology and life. Now they are both adamant at least that their lives might be orchestrated in some way - not necessarily by authorship, but certainly manipulated to some degree nonetheless. They start becoming suspicious of the other characters, although they do try to keep making points - and this is when the show seems to take notice. The other characters start looking at them, almost invariably, like they're unhealthily obsessive, and they start developing feelings of isolation - but at least they have each other. The season finale ends with a breakup/falling-out with another character, making one/both of them feel...pretty sad. One other character starts being curious about why they feel that way so much.
  • Season 6: This drives one of them to start doing research about theirself. Notice that they are using a made-up search-engine that specifically excludes results about the show they're in, but they don't imagine this could actually be the case. This slowly progresses until the finale, with a lot of obstacles facing their personal life in the process, and eventually they finally come across a picture of theirself on the internet...over another person's name. The credits instantly transition from it in that way, showing other pictures of characters over actors' names.
  • Season 7: At this point, the show banishes both of them under the explanation by other characters that they're caught-up in a silly conspiracy, and uses this time to introduce an old enemy of theirs (either from childhood or adulthood) into the cast. Once the enemy is properly friends with the main cast, finally in the finale, one of them shows up and makes a frantic, obviously drawn-out declaration that they are definitely, in fact, in a soap-opera. A different actor is playing them, effectively overwriting everyone's memories of their appearance - they will probably have the most actor-replacements out of every character over the course of the show - but this transition is in vain. They would say it straight-up, but they realise they wouldn't be believed without quite a few lines of pre-explanation, and it reïnforces their assimilation into a soap-opera. The episode ends with the enemy walking into the room.
  • Season 8: The first episode starts on some parts of storylines that aren't that, but quickly goes back to the revelation-scene since it knows it can't hide it. The enemy is pretty much like, "long time no see... Looks like you've only started sucking more since we last saw each other. We're on a soap-opera? Really??? Wow, you're pretty mental LOL." The person is like "no I have proof" but the enemy doesn't want to hear about it. In the next episode, the person tries to be more and more desperate so that the enemy's consistent stubbornness to not see the evidence proves their fabricated melodrama, but they just leave instead, with the person unable to do anything about it. They get as little screen-time as they can get during the season, but eventually more people see the evidence, and this is when some of them start trying to contact people in the real world through various websites trying to make sense of the conundrum (although, as someone with more technological experiënce will note early in season 10, all of their devices have been blocked from accessing most sites mentioning the show, including almost every mainstream social platform). One or two characters die, and in the season finale they're all contemplating what to do about the situation.
  • Season 9: At this point, the cast is very worried about what's happening. Problems seem to happen more frequently, and they're all trying to take steps to minimise the risks that would be imposed upon their lives since every little thing has arbitrary meaning while they all try to come up with a long-term plan to deal with the situation. They try to do things like be extremely upfront, minimise conflict, become friends, leave the show and be more diligent, but it's still very hard, and a lot of characters feel oppression and existential crisis. More family-members and other characters with issues join the cast in an attempt to distract everyone from the reälity of the situation, but this is also in vain as the main cast fills them in and reïnforces the main problem. At the end of the season, a prominent character gets into serious trouble (with the law or a dodgy group of people), and another character sets out to research the producers.
  • Season 10: Now the show is becoming very dramatic and violent. Characters are suddenly reminded of previous crimes they'd committed and other things they regret doing or ought to do, facing significantly more problems, and even dying more frequently. Someone notes that everyone's devices have been restricted, so they go to places like the local libraries and other places with devices...but they're all restricted as well, so they try to call family-members overseas, telling them about their research so they can continue for them. As usual, it takes a long time to get through to them, and most of them still think they're crazy and don't bother to do any research, but a few believe them and start doing research...but one of them die as well. Which makes the relatives in the cast feel really guilty for dragging their distant family into this. (BTW, I don't think convincing deepfake-technology existed when I first wrote all this.) It's now at this point that a few overseas characters start trying to talk to people in the real world through the internet, and a few sentiments are shared with the main cast. In the season finale, one of the characters snaps and runs outside, and many other characters chase them, forcing the show's footage to become akin to a frantic scene in a reälity show with freehand cinematography. In one improvised take shot from two different angles, the character who ran outside starts yelling at people, desperately trying to spread the word about their situation, while the others follow them. Eventually, they see someone who may or may not know them as an actor or a character on the show, but tells them to stop harassing them and get that camera away from them. The character looks around, unable to see the camera, but they tell them to stop playing games and point at the camera. A lot of this might not happen, but the important thing is this: still unable to perceive the camera, the person runs toward the screen, looks indirectly at the viewer, and says something like, "are you there? Stop watching and HELP ME!" Then it cuts to black.
  • Season 11: The first episode starts with the other characters chasing him for a while, but the cinematography is now steady again, indicating that the show is taking back control. They see him hit by a truck. Of course. The show is now flashing faint, subliminal messages on the screen such as "KEEP WATCHING", "THIS IS A GOOD SHOW", "THE UNCERTAINTY IS CAPTIVATING", "THIS IS WORTH WATCHING", "WE NEED MORE OF THIS" and "STAY WITH THE SHOW" multiple times per episode, particularly at the end of each one, and the soundtrack also has subliminal lyrics/voices similarly raving about the show. It also uses other propaganda and psychological techniques to simultaneously coax the viewers into watching the show and ignoring the problem. (Actually do this much later; the show should not be that desperate yet.) System

  • Most of the show's production outside of filming happens in a private online organisation with people all over the world using high-security physical and digital setups in fortified locations and wearing full-body covers like Zerozero0000, and if any crew-member is found by any character, they immediately leave the production-crew and destroy their computer, along with the facility if few enough characters arrive and the crew-member manages to escape, in addition to being warned to relocate by any camera-crew who are filming characters approaching their location or finding out where they are
  • If the show ends prematurely and they still get to continue production before it's imminent conclusion, most characters die, and the few that manage to escape and gain freedom do not get a happy ending

Strategies of the cast

  • Faking their own deaths and then actively trying to stay away from the cast so they can be free
    • On at least one occasion, the show makes the cast meet up with them by compelling them to go somewhere that the falsely-deceased happens to be
  • Deliberately doing things that would make the show less popular, such as:
    • Act out-of-character or inconsistent
    • Be tasteless
      • Raise the show's classification to increasingly mature audiënces
      • Say dogmatic, extremist, religious, political and hateful things that would get the production-crew in trouble and prevent the show from being aired on television or maintained on video-sharing sites
      • Force people to not take it seriously by acting silly, stupid, unfunny and deliberately obtuse
      • Research a combination of cringy, brainrotten, offensive and dead memes that different groups of people hate and overdo them during any situation that seems significant (an entire episode might just be characters taking deep breaths, bracing themselves and then saying skibidi ohio rizz chungus sussus amogus uganda knuckles mlg trump for up to half the runtime in total while trying to get out of a disaster)
        • This would probably be thwarted by making the most egregious person die and vindicating the audiënce
      • Develop a cryptolang where most of the words are replaced by such memes
    • Coördinate to suddenly change the genre at random but long intervals to minimise intersecting demographics (for example, they may suddenly act like a bunch of bogans, then act like a group of high-society upper-class citizens, then act like a bunch of casual nerds, geeks and dorks in a sitcom)
    • Privately research the actors of other characters (not themselves) to try to incentivise them to do something to help the other characters when not filming
    • Find and watch their own show to try to learn about the filming and production style and techniques, and then strategically try to exploit them
    • Privately research the people involved in the show's production, find out as many heinous things they've done as they can and try to expose them on live TV
      • It would look kinda cool if the end-credits zoomed-out in one episode and transitioned to someone's screen as they prepared to start doing this
  • Trying to deconstruct the tropes employed
    • Realising they're talking as though they're playing into recipients' suspicions (e.g. saying lots of loaded statements they don't know the meaning of, persistently acting threatening until the moment they reveal their real intentions), and then trying to figure out what dramatic irony they're in and what expectations the screenwriters would be trying to subvert
  • Barricading themselves into a tiny, cramped space with no room for another person so they can try to talk without being filmed
  • Locking themselves in a small one-way room with a low ceiling and no windows and waving their arms around trying to see if they can bump into any invisible cameras or camera-crew, with the room connected to a narrow corridor that one character stays outside, walking forward with their limbs stretched-out and trying to catch or force-away the crew to prevent them from just filming the silly noise from outside
  • Realising the main genre of the show is psychological horror in addition to drama and trying to subvert it somehow to bore the audiënce
  • Deliberately trying to become and remain hopeless so that the show becomes so grimdark and depressing that the audiënce gets bored and loses investment
    • And then continuing to fight by actively refusing to participate in all the opportunities for good times the show suddenly starts throwing at them to try to counteract this, which eventually leads to the show trying to introduce counsellors, therapists, psychiatrists and supportive friends to uplift them, and which fails anyway because sitting around being miserable is no way to live and people would rather press the shock-button than do nothing in an otherwise empty room (and now we just ended up with more characters to drag into this mess)
    • The show also tries to counteract this by abridging all of the boring parts where they just sit around being sad and doing things that are boring for the plot, while the characters wait and wait in the vain hope that if they do it for long enough, the show will have to give up before it gets too boring to continue
  • Playing along to see if non-resistance and pretending they don't have a metafictive problem makes everything easier
  • Collectively trying to part ways with each other to completely prevent interpersonal interactions
    • This probably won't work because most people would care about each other too much to leave, and choose oppression with bonding over separation with freedom, and the few people who do leave just serve as a catalyst for more drama for the remaining cast

    At some point one character might get that crazy idea, dramatically announce that they got it (knowing the audiënce and hopefully the creätors won't know what it is), and start trying to give characters ideas when they're not on-screen. You can kind of see subtle, mildly-suspicious shifts in the characters' demeanours until they're all in on it, until it all culminates in a pact including most if not the whole cast about something unspoken.

    The idea is to effectively recurse the soap-opera by engaging in pretend-conflicts in order to trick the audiënce so that genuine diëgetic conflicts don't happen (because they are no longer forced to happen for the show to remain engaging) and tragedies are averted. At first you don't notice - they decide to bide their time before initiating the first pretend conflict, because the cast will have a well-established sense of solidarity by whatever season this would be, and they won't want to look too suspicious. In the meantime, if they have any other unanimous plans to rebel against the show, they try to slightly exaggerate/put a focus on them to make it even less obvious to the audiënce what the plan is.

    The difference is that rather than pretending to be fictional characters, they have to pretend to be themselves. It's not long before characters start making Freudian slips, trying to continue as though everything is all still natural. Some characters find that engaging in pretend conflicts is actually stressful and seems like it is probably going to escalate into genuine conflicts, while others find that it actually helps them deescelate and become earnest social buffers since they are engaging in conflicts as though they don't really need to exist, and can stop existing at any time.

    Of course, this backfires because the cast is overall becoming increasingly chill and unwilling to engage in genuine conflicts, and you can tell that there's something off about their performance. That means the show doubles down and starts creäting genuine diëgetic conflicts even worse than before
